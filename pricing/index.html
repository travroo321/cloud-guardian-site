The call

Day 0 · 15 minutes of your time

You call, or you fill in the form. Somebody who fixes things answers, not a qualifier reading a script.

We ask what broke, what it costs you when it breaks, and who has been looking after it. If we are not the right fit we say so on that call rather than three meetings later. Nothing is signed and nothing is sold.

What you do: talk for fifteen minutes.

02 🔍

The assessment

Day 1 to 7 · Two hours of access

A full audit of what you actually have. Free, and genuinely no obligation.

Every device, every account, every licence, every exposure. We scan what is reachable from the internet, check whether your credentials are already circulating in breach data, and inventory what you are paying for against what you are using. Most of it runs in the background while you work.

What you do: give us read access for a couple of hours.

03 📋

The findings

Day 7 · One meeting

You get the written report. You keep it whether you hire us or not.

Prioritised by what would actually hurt, not by what is easiest for us to sell. It names the gaps, the wasted spend, and what each fix costs. Plenty of prospects have taken that document to their existing provider and used it to get the work done there. That is a fair outcome, and it is why the report is yours.

What you do: read it, and decide.

Remediation

Week 2 to 6 · Mostly invisible

We close the worst gaps first, in the order the report set out.

Cutovers happen outside your hours. Nothing changes without you knowing what and why. We also cut the waste the audit found, and for most clients the licensing and cloud savings offset a real share of what they pay us. Your old provider gets handled professionally; you do not have to have that conversation.

What you do: approve the plan, then get on with your work.

A named engineer who knows your environment rather than whoever picks up. Documentation delivered to you and kept current, because it was always yours. One monthly figure that does not move unless your headcount does. And if you ever leave, you leave with everything, which is the part most providers make difficult on purpose.

What you do: call us when something breaks. That is the whole job.

Pricing FAQ

THE QUESTIONS
BEHIND THE NUMBER

How can you be half the price of my current provider?

Because it is a different cost structure, not a discount. A legacy MSP carries a tiered help desk where a ticket passes through two people who cannot fix it before reaching one who can, on tooling bought a decade ago. We automate the volume work and use AI to triage, correlate and close the noise, so we carry far less labour per managed endpoint. That difference goes into the price rather than into a margin. During the assessment we show you the line items so you can check it against your current invoice.

Is there a minimum number of users?

No. Most providers set a floor at 10 or 20 seats because small accounts are unprofitable under a staffed model. Ours is not, so there is no floor.

What is the contract term?

Silver has no contract at all, because there is nothing to contract for: you call, we bill the time. Gold and Platinum are typically annual because the onboarding work is front-loaded, but there is no three-year lock and no exit fee. If you leave, your documentation goes with you, because it was always yours.

What is not included?

On Gold and Platinum, all Cloud Guardian security tooling and its licensing sits inside the seat price. What stays outside every plan is your own third-party licensing such as Microsoft 365 or Google Workspace seats, hardware, and project work such as cabling and wireless surveys. The Small Business Plan is narrower by design: it protects the email you already have, so the wider suite and its licensing are not part of it. On Silver, after hours and emergency work is charged at a special rate, agreed with you before the work happens rather than discovered on an invoice afterwards. All of it is quoted before it happens. You will not find a line on an invoice you have not already seen.

Why is Silver hourly when Gold and Platinum are flat?

Because they are different deals. On Silver you are buying our time when you need it, which suits a business that has few problems and would rather not pay a retainer for the privilege of not having them. Billing in 15 minute increments matters here: $90 an hour works out to $22.50 per 15 minutes, so a ten minute fix costs $22.50, not a rounded up hour, and any call under 5 minutes is free, even a support call. On Gold and Platinum you are buying the absence of problems, so the incentive has to be on us. A flat number means we lose money when your environment is badly run, which is the correct way round.

Do the prices change if we grow?

Per user, so yes, proportionally. Adding people changes the figure on the next cycle. Nothing else moves without a conversation first.

What does the free assessment actually involve?

A full inventory of devices, accounts, licensing and exposure, an external check for what is already reachable from the internet, and a search for your credentials in breach data. You get written findings with prioritised remediation. If you do not hire us, you keep the report and can hand it to whoever you do hire.
