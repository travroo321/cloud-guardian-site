Local Government · Reality Check

WHY PUBLIC IT
IS DIFFERENT

The constraints a private-sector provider tends to discover too late.

01 🗳️

Procurement rules, not purchase orders

Thresholds, quote requirements, formal bids, and cooperative purchasing all change how work has to be packaged. A vendor who does not understand this either cannot be paid or puts the entity's award at risk. We scope work to fit the procurement path you actually have to use.

02 📄

Everything is potentially public

Assessment findings, invoices, and correspondence can be subject to an OPRA request. Security documentation has to be written so that the sensitive technical detail is separable from the record, which is a discipline most providers have never had to apply.

03 🔐

Ransomware against essential services

Municipal ransomware is not an inconvenience, it stops tax collection, court operations, permitting, dispatch support, and utility billing. The recovery plan has to answer how the town operates while systems are rebuilt, and it has to be written before it is needed.

04 👥

One IT person, or none

Many entities run on a single technician or a part-time contractor with no coverage, no documentation, and no succession plan. When that person is unavailable, nothing about the environment is known.

Local Government FAQ

QUESTIONS WE GET
FROM LOCAL GOVERNMENT CLIENTS

Do you respond to public RFPs and bids?

Yes. Send the solicitation and we will confirm within one business day whether we are bidding. We submit complete, responsive packages with every required form and certificate, in the format the specification asks for.

Are you registered and insured to work for a New Jersey public entity?

Yes. We maintain New Jersey business registration and carry general liability, professional liability, and workers compensation coverage, and we provide certificates with additional insured endorsements when required. We also supply pay-to-play, non-collusion, Chapter 271, and EEO documentation as part of a submission.

Can you present to a governing body or committee?

Yes. We attend council, board, and committee meetings, present the scope in plain language for a public audience, and answer questions on the record.

What kinds of public entities do you work with?

Municipalities, municipal utilities authorities, sewerage and water authorities, boards of education, fire districts, libraries, housing authorities, and county-level agencies.

How do you handle a ransomware incident at a municipality?

With a plan written in advance that assumes essential services must continue. That means a documented manual fallback for tax collection, permitting, and billing, tested restores with a known recovery time, defined notification and reporting steps, and a single accountable point of contact through the incident.

Can you work alongside our existing IT person?

Yes, and it is often the best structure. Your technician keeps day-to-day familiarity while we provide coverage, specialization, monitoring, security work, and documentation, which also solves the single-point-of-failure problem.
