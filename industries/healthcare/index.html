Healthcare and Dental · Reality Check

WHERE CLINICAL IT
FALLS APART

Patterns we find in nearly every practice assessment.

01 🖥️

Imaging on an unsupported operating system

Sensor, panoramic, CBCT, and ultrasound software frequently ships tied to an OS version the vendor never recertified. That machine ends up unpatched and on the same flat network as everything else, which turns one imaging workstation into the whole practice's exposure.

02 💾

Backups nobody has restored

The EHR vendor backs up the EHR. Nobody backs up the imaging archive, the scanned intake forms, the practice's shared drive, or the machine running the insurance clearinghouse. A restore has never been attempted. That is the gap that turns a hardware failure into weeks of reconstruction.

03 👤

Shared logins at the front desk

One account, one password taped inside a drawer, five people using it. Audit logging becomes meaningless the moment you cannot attribute an access to a person, and that is precisely what the Security Rule expects you to be able to do.

04 📱

Personal phones with patient data on them

Staff photographing a chart or a wound, texting a colleague about a patient, or accessing email on an unmanaged device. No enrollment, no encryption requirement, no remote wipe when the phone is lost or the employee leaves.

How We Handle It

THE SECURITY RULE, AS CONFIGURATION

Controls, not adjectives. Each of these is something we configure, document, and can show you evidence of.

  • 🔐
    Unique user identification and access controlNamed accounts, role-based permission, multi-factor authentication, and automatic logoff on clinical workstations. §164.312(a).
  • 📋
    Audit controlsAccess and activity logging on systems holding electronic protected health information, retained and reviewable. §164.312(b).
  • 🔒
    Encryption at rest and in transitFull-disk encryption on every endpoint and laptop, TLS on transmission, encrypted backup targets. §164.312(a)(2)(iv) and (e)(2)(ii).
  • 🏥
    Contingency and recoveryTested restores, documented downtime procedure, and a recovery objective the practice has actually agreed to rather than one assumed by default.

Healthcare and Dental FAQ

QUESTIONS WE GET
FROM HEALTHCARE AND DENTAL CLIENTS

Will you sign a Business Associate Agreement?

Yes, before we access anything. Any IT provider with access to systems holding protected health information is a business associate under HIPAA, and any provider who hesitates on a BAA is telling you something important.

Can you make our practice HIPAA compliant?

No provider can. Compliance is an organizational program covering administrative, physical, and technical safeguards, and parts of it are policy and training work that belongs to the practice. What we do is implement and evidence the technical safeguards, and hand you documentation your compliance program and any auditor can use. Anyone selling you a HIPAA compliance certificate is selling you nothing.

Our imaging software only runs on an old Windows version. What now?

We isolate it. The machine goes on its own network segment with tightly restricted traffic, application allowlisting, no general internet or email on it, and monitoring around it. That contains the risk while you plan the vendor upgrade, instead of pretending the risk is not there.

Do you support dental practices specifically?

Yes. Dental brings its own stack: Dentrix, Eaglesoft, Open Dental, Curve, along with sensor and CBCT imaging that is unusually sensitive to network and workstation changes. Operatory cabling and reliable chairside wireless are also frequent projects for us.

How fast can you get onsite if the schedule is stopped?

Middlesex and Somerset County practices are typically same business day and often same hour. Remote response is immediate, 24/7.
