Law Firms · Reality Check

WHAT ACTUALLY
BREAKS AT A FIRM

These are the calls we get from firms switching to us, in order of how often we hear them.

01 🗂️

The document management system crawls

Scanned PDFs, deposition video, and twenty years of matter files sitting on a server that was sized for a much smaller firm. Search takes forty seconds, so nobody searches, so documents get re-created and the same file exists in six versions with no authority.

02 💰

Trust accounting nobody will touch

IOLTA reconciliation runs on a workstation under someone's desk that has never been backed up in a way anyone has tested. Ethics exposure sitting on consumer hardware is the single most common finding in our legal audits.

03 🎯

The firm is a named ransomware target

Legal is targeted deliberately: attackers know a firm holds counterparties' confidential material and faces a hard deadline calendar. IBM puts the 2024 global average breach cost at $4.88 million, and the leverage against a firm is not just the data, it is the notification obligation to every client whose matter was touched.

04 📅

Deadline-day outages

E-filing portals, court connections, and the one paralegal's machine that has the certificate installed. When it fails at 4pm on a filing deadline, a ticket queue is not a plan.

How We Handle It

CONFIDENTIALITY, IN TECHNICAL TERMS

Controls, not adjectives. Each of these is something we configure, document, and can show you evidence of.

  • 🔐
    Access on a need-to-know basisMatter-level and folder-level permissions so a paralegal on one matter cannot browse an unrelated sensitive file. Ethical walls that are enforced by the system, not by a memo.
  • 📝
    Logging that survives a questionWho opened what, when, and from where. If a client or a bar inquiry ever asks, the answer is a report, not a recollection.
  • 🔓
    Offboarding that actually revokesAccess terminated the day someone leaves, across email, document management, practice management, VPN, and phone. Written checklist, executed and recorded.
  • ♻️
    Recovery you have watched workBackups are worthless until a restore has been performed in front of you. We test restores and give you the evidence, because a backup nobody has restored is a hope, not a control.

Law Firms FAQ

QUESTIONS WE GET
FROM LAW FIRMS CLIENTS

Which practice management platforms do you support?

Clio, MyCase, Smokeball, Actionstep, Filevine, CosmoLex, TimeSolv, Rocket Matter, PracticePanther, Zola Suite, and older on-premise systems including PCLaw, Tabs3, and Amicus Attorney. On the document side we work with NetDocuments, iManage, Worldox, and SharePoint-based structures. We are platform-neutral: we support what you run, and we will tell you honestly if what you run is the problem.

We are only twelve people. Are we too small for you?

Firms of 5 to 50 users are our core market. Larger providers treat a twelve-attorney firm as overhead. We size the engagement to the firm, which is exactly why our pricing lands near half of what a legacy MSP quotes for the same coverage.

Can you help us answer a client security questionnaire?

Yes. Corporate clients increasingly send outside counsel guidelines and security questionnaires. We produce the technical evidence those documents ask for: encryption posture, access controls, logging, backup verification, incident response procedure, and vendor list. We are not your ethics counsel, but we supply what your answers have to rest on.

What happens to our current IT provider's knowledge?

We audit the environment before cutover and document it ourselves, so nothing depends on the outgoing provider being cooperative. Most firm transitions complete in 30 to 60 days.

Do you support remote and hybrid attorneys?

Yes. Secure remote access, cloud document access, and a phone system where the firm's number follows the attorney to home, court, or car without forwarding tricks.
