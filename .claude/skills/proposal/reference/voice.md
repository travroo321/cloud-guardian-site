# How Cloud Guardian writes

The proposal has to sound like the website. The site's voice is plain, direct
and slightly blunt — it wins by refusing to do the things prospects expect an
MSP to do. Match it, or the proposal reads like it came from someone else.

## The shape of it

**Say the number.** The whole site is built on publishing prices most MSPs
hide. "Most IT providers will not put a number on a page. That is not because
pricing is complicated; it is because the number is hard to defend once you see
it next to what you get."

**Concede the real thing.** The site tells prospects to take the free
assessment report to their current provider if they want. That confidence is
the tone. A proposal that oversells loses it.

**Short declaratives. Then one that lands.** "Nothing is signed and nothing is
sold." "That is the whole job." "It was always yours."

**Explain the mechanism, not the adjective.** Not "world-class security" but
"phishing and impersonation defense, SPF, DKIM and DMARC enforced." Not
"proactive monitoring" but what actually happens when something fires.

**Name the trade honestly.** The site says what each plan does *not* include
in the same list as what it does. Do the same.

## Words the site uses

Second person. "You call, we bill the time." Contractions are rare — the site
writes "do not" and "is not" more often than "don't" and "isn't". US spelling
throughout.

Compound modifiers are hyphenated: 15-minute blocks, after-hours work,
full-time user, one-hour minimum.

Numbers: spell out under ten, numerals from ten up. "About seven minutes",
"15 minutes", "30 seconds".

## Do not write

- "Leverage", "synergy", "best-in-class", "cutting-edge", "peace of mind",
  "trusted partner", "in today's fast-paced world".
- Fear-first openings. The site leads with what it costs and what you get, not
  with ransomware statistics.
- Claims of certifications, partnerships, client names or case studies that
  were not given to you. If you want a proof point and do not have one, leave
  it out.
- Anything implying a long contract. There is no three-year lock.

## Reusable lines from the site

These are already in the company's voice and are safe to adapt:

- "Twice the quality. Half the price." — the standing tagline. Note it now sits
  against a $110 Gold price; use it only where it still reads as defensible.
- "No minimum seat count, no three-year term, no charge to leave. If we are not
  earning it, you should be able to go."
- "A named engineer who knows your environment rather than whoever picks up."
- "Documentation delivered to you and kept current, because it was always
  yours."
- "If you leave, you leave with everything, which is the part most providers
  make difficult on purpose."
- "You will not find a line on an invoice you have not already seen."
- "Prioritised by what would actually hurt, not by what is easiest for us to
  sell."

## Industry framing

Match the security and compliance page to what the prospect actually faces.
Draw the framing from the matching page under `/industries/` when one exists —
those pages are already written in the right voice and name the real
obligations.

| Prospect type | What to lead with |
|---|---|
| Healthcare, dental, benefits | HIPAA, PHI, business associate obligations |
| Law firms | Client confidentiality, court deadlines, practice management systems |
| Accounting, finance | WISP requirements, IRS Publication 4557, client PII |
| Retail, hospitality | PCI DSS, point-of-sale exposure, seasonal staff turnover |
| Manufacturing, warehouse | Uptime, OT and office network separation, shift coverage |
| Real estate | Wire fraud and impersonation, application data retention |
| Nonprofits | Donor data, grant reporting, licensing costs recovered |
| Local government | Public records, resident data, procurement |
| Education | FERPA, student devices, staff turnover |

If the prospect does not fit any of these, write to what their website actually
shows they do. Do not force a vertical.
